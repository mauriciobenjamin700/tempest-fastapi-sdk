    QR code with a logo and a frame (ride link)¶

    A QR code printed on the car window, on a poster at the bus station or in an image posted on a WhatsApp status takes the passenger straight to the ride. With the brand's logo in the middle, it also says whose link it is before anyone points a camera at it. And in a frame with the driver's photo and name, the route and the written link, the image explains itself.

    The logo covers modules of the QR code. Error correction makes up for that: level H recovers up to 30% of the symbol. tempest_fastapi_sdk.share always generates the QR code at level H, pastes the logo on a white frame at a size that was measured to keep reading, and refuses a logo fraction above the measured ceiling.

    By the end you will have:

    • render_qr(url, logo=...): the PNG of the QR code with the logo in the middle;
    • ShareFrameRenderer: the frame in two shapes, vertical for the status and square for a chat or a group;
    • GET /api/caronas/{trip_id}/qr.png and GET /api/caronas/{trip_id}/moldura.png, pointing at the ride's share page.

    Installation¶

    The QR code is generated by segno (pure Python, no dependencies) and drawn with Pillow. Both come with the [image] extra:

    uv add "tempest-fastapi-sdk[image]>=0.371.0"

    The frame writes text, so it needs a TrueType font on the machine, like the preview card. See Font in Docker.

    Step 1: the QR code with the logo¶

    from pathlib import Path

from tempest_fastapi_sdk.share import render_qr

png: bytes = render_qr(
    "https://app.buscar.com.br/s/42",
    logo=Path("static/logo.png"),
)
Path("qr.png").write_bytes(png)

    The result, with a 400×400 logo:

    What render_qr does for you:

    • Level H, always. Left alone, segno 1.6.6 picked L (7% recovery) for this recipe's links, and L leaves no redundancy for the logo.
    • Never Micro QR. segno picks Micro QR for very short text (for "42" it produced an M1), and phone readers generally do not support it.
    • Logo at 25% of the QR side, sized against the QR code without its border, keeping its aspect ratio, on a one-module white frame. The frame separates the logo from the modules around it.
    • A 35% ceiling. logo_fraction above MAX_QR_LOGO_FRACTION raises ValueError before drawing.

    The other parameters: scale is pixels per module. For large prints, raise scale instead of enlarging the finished PNG, because enlarging blurs the module edges. border is the white margin in modules, and 4 is what the standard asks for.

    How much logo fits¶

    Measured with segno 1.6.6 and zxing-cpp 3.1.1. Setup: level H QR code, scale=10, square opaque logo on a white frame, and two links, one of 30 characters (QR version 4, 33×33 modules) and one of 64 (version 7, 45×45). "Degraded" is the image shrunk and blurred, in four combinations (shrinking from ½ to ¼, Gaussian blur from 0.6 to 1.0 px), to imitate a camera photo:

    Logo side (fraction of the QR) Area covered 30-char link: clean / degraded 64-char link: clean / degraded
    20% 4% reads / 4 of 4 reads / 4 of 4
    25% 6% reads / 4 of 4 reads / 4 of 4
    30% 9% reads / 4 of 4 reads / 4 of 4
    35% 12% reads / 4 of 4 reads / 4 of 4
    40% 16% fails / 0 of 4 reads / 2 of 4
    45% 20% fails / 0 of 4 fails / 0 of 4

    Hence the default DEFAULT_QR_LOGO_FRACTION = 0.25, three steps below the first failure (40%), and the ceiling MAX_QR_LOGO_FRACTION = 0.35, the last value that read in every case. The default's margin is for what the measurement does not reproduce: glare on the window, low-resolution printing, an old phone camera. The test tests/share/test_qr.py decodes the QR code at the default and at the ceiling, for both links, and confirms that 40% stops reading the short link.

    This measures a reader, not a camera

    The table was measured by decoding the image with zxing-cpp, not by pointing a phone at it. Before printing in bulk, test the final QR code with two or three different cameras, at the distance and in the light where it will be read.

    Two choices outside the logo matter too:

    • Link length. At the same printed size, more modules are smaller modules, and the camera has to come closer. Prefer /s/42 to a URL with a UUID and campaign parameters.
    • Colors. The table was measured with black modules on a white background, which is what render_qr draws. Put the brand color in the logo.

    Step 2: the frame to share¶

    The frame is the image the driver posts. It carries its own way back: the QR code, for whoever sees the image on another screen, and the written link, for whoever cannot scan.

    from pathlib import Path

from tempest_fastapi_sdk.share import (
    ShareFrameLayoutEnum,
    ShareFrameRenderer,
    ShareFrameSchema,
)

renderer = ShareFrameRenderer(
    background=("#0b3d91", "#061f4a"),
    accent_color="#ffd166",
    logo=Path("static/logo.png"),
)

frame = ShareFrameSchema(
    url="https://app.buscar.com.br/s/42",
    title="Ana Souza",
    subtitle="Teresina → Fortaleza · Friday, 6pm",
    brand="BusCar",
    caption="Scan and book your seat",
)

story: bytes = renderer.render(
    frame,
    layout=ShareFrameLayoutEnum.STORY,
    photo=Path("ana.jpg").read_bytes(),
)
square: bytes = renderer.render(
    frame,
    layout=ShareFrameLayoutEnum.SQUARE,
    photo=Path("ana.jpg").read_bytes(),
)
    Vertical (STORY, 1080×1920), for the status Square (SQUARE, 1080×1080), for a chat or a group

    (The images show the Portuguese version of the same frame.)

    Piece by piece:

    • ShareFrameRenderer takes the same arguments as the preview's ShareCardRenderer: colors, logo, font_path. Use the same colors on both, and the card and the frame look alike. An invalid color, a broken logo and a missing font fail at construction.
    • The logo shows up twice: at the top, next to brand, and in the middle of the QR code, under the same rule as render_qr.
    • photo is the person's photo, as bytes. It is cropped from the center into a circle, ringed in the accent color, with the EXIF orientation applied. Without a photo, the circle carries the initials of title ("Ana Souza" becomes AS). A photo that does not open, or has more than MAX_PHOTO_PIXELS, raises ValueError.
    • show_url=True writes the link without https:// under the QR code.
    • Long text shrinks, then is cut with …, and never leaves the margin. A line break becomes a space.

    The test tests/share/test_frame.py decodes the QR code inside the frame in both shapes: with and without a photo, with and without a logo, with long text and with a UUID link.

    Why the photo comes from your service

    photo is bytes, not a URL. Fetching on the server a URL that came from the client opens the door to SSRF (the server making requests wherever the attacker wants). Read the photo from your storage, where it was already validated at upload.

    Step 3: the ride's routes¶

    The QR code points at the ride's share page (/s/{id}, from the Sharing with a preview recipe), not at the app directly. Whoever scans it lands in the browser, and the page takes them to the app. The same link, pasted into WhatsApp, shows the preview with the card.

    import asyncio
from pathlib import Path

from fastapi import FastAPI, HTTPException, Response

from tempest_fastapi_sdk.share import (
    ShareFrameLayoutEnum,
    ShareFrameRenderer,
    ShareFrameSchema,
    render_qr,
)

PUBLIC_URL: str = "https://app.buscar.com.br"
LOGO: Path = Path("static/logo.png")
TRIPS: dict[str, dict[str, str]] = {
    "42": {
        "driver": "Ana Souza",
        "route": "Teresina → Fortaleza · Friday, 6pm",
        "photo": "static/ana.jpg",
    },
}

renderer = ShareFrameRenderer(
    background=("#0b3d91", "#061f4a"),
    accent_color="#ffd166",
    logo=LOGO,
)
app = FastAPI()


def png_response(png: bytes) -> Response:
    return Response(
        png,
        media_type="image/png",
        headers={"Cache-Control": "public, max-age=3600"},
    )


@app.get("/api/caronas/{trip_id}/qr.png", response_class=Response)
async def trip_qr(trip_id: str) -> Response:
    if trip_id not in TRIPS:
        raise HTTPException(status_code=404)
    png = await asyncio.to_thread(render_qr, f"{PUBLIC_URL}/s/{trip_id}", logo=LOGO)
    return png_response(png)


@app.get("/api/caronas/{trip_id}/moldura.png", response_class=Response)
async def trip_frame(
    trip_id: str,
    layout: ShareFrameLayoutEnum = ShareFrameLayoutEnum.STORY,
) -> Response:
    trip = TRIPS.get(trip_id)
    if trip is None:
        raise HTTPException(status_code=404)
    frame = ShareFrameSchema(
        url=f"{PUBLIC_URL}/s/{trip_id}",
        title=trip["driver"],
        subtitle=trip["route"],
        brand="BusCar",
        caption="Scan and book your seat",
    )
    photo = Path(trip["photo"]).read_bytes()
    png = await asyncio.to_thread(renderer.render, frame, layout=layout, photo=photo)
    return png_response(png)

    The routes live under /api/ on purpose. Behind the nginx of the share page, only /s/ and /api/ reach the backend, with ^~. Outside them, a path ending in .png lands in the SPA's static block. Measured on nginx:alpine 1.31.9: /caronas/42/qr.png gave 404, and /api/caronas/42/qr.png gave 200 image/png.

    GET /api/caronas/42/moldura.png returns the vertical frame, and GET /api/caronas/42/moldura.png?layout=square the square one. FastAPI validates layout against the enum and answers 422 for any other value.

    Off the event loop. Drawing the frame took about 105 ms (mean of 10, vertical, with photo and logo, Pillow 13.0.0, on the SDK's development machine). The QR code alone took about 13 ms (mean of 50, 64-character link, logo read from disk on every call). Both go through asyncio.to_thread, so the loop keeps serving other requests.

    Short cache. The frame changes when the ride changes: time, photo, driver. One hour of Cache-Control limits how long a stale image circulates. If your model has updated_at, add ?v=<updated_at> to the image link, as the share page does with the card.

    Recap¶

    • render_qr(url, logo=...) generates the QR code at level H, without Micro QR, with the logo at 25% of the side on a white frame. Above 35% it refuses: 40% already failed in the measurement.
    • ShareFrameRenderer.render(frame, layout=..., photo=...) draws the frame: logo and brand, round photo (or the initials), name, route, the QR code on a white panel, the written link and the call to action. STORY (1080×1920) is for the status, SQUARE (1080×1080) for a chat or a group.
    • The QR code points at /s/{id}: whoever scans it reaches the app, and the same link has a preview on WhatsApp.
    • The photo comes from your storage, as bytes, never from a URL fetched on the server.
    • Before printing, test with real cameras.
